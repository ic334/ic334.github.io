The one I still tell people about

A uniqueness test started failing on duplicate surrogate keys and nobody could work out why. The cause: our key scheme hadn't been built for a client reaching their millionth record, and that record landed in the morning refresh, making two different rows collide. I wrote a replacement surrogate‑key macro modeled on dbt_utils and rolled it — along with the other macros I'd written for recurring messy‑data problems — into an internal package every client project imported.

Spotify Audio Analytics

Personal project
dbt + DuckDB · ~30k tracks

What it shows

A production-shaped transformation layer: staging → a tested, documented star schema → reporting models, with 23 data-quality tests and a browsable lineage graph. The raw data repeats a track once per playlist it appears on, so staging picks a grain — one row per track — and a uniqueness test enforces it; playlist-level questions would need a separate bridge table. Runs end to end locally, no warehouse and no credentials.

What the data showed

Average valence — how “happy” a track sounds — fell from 0.60 in 2010 to 0.45 by 2016 and stayed there through 2019, while energy barely moved. And energy doesn't buy popularity: EDM is the most energetic genre in the set (0.81) and the least popular (30.7), while Pop is calmer (0.70) and the most popular (45.9). Popularity is a 2020 snapshot, so it favors recent releases.
